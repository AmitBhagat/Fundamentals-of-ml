Chapter 64: Chi-Square Test

Prerequisite

  • Probability Distributions: A solid grasp of the Normal distribution and the concept of "sampling distribution."
  • Hypothesis Testing: Understanding the Null Hypothesis (H0H_0H0​), Alternative Hypothesis (HaH_aHa​), and the ppp-value.
  • Categorical Data: Distinction between nominal/ordinal variables and continuous numerical data.

Analogy

When you sit down to renew your car insurance, you aren't just blindly paying a bill; you are performing a mental audit. You have an internal expectation of what the premium "should" look like based on your clean driving record, the age of your vehicle, and market trends. However, the quote the insurance company sends over—the "observed" reality—rarely aligns perfectly with your "expected" reality.

The Chi-Square test is essentially the mathematical equivalent of that audit. It measures the friction between what you expected to see and what actually showed up in your inbox. If the gap between your expected quote and the observed quote is small, you chalk it up to minor market fluctuations. But if that gap is massive, you know something fundamental has changed—perhaps your "no-claim" status wasn't applied, or the car's value was miscalculated. The test quantifies this discrepancy to tell you if the difference is just "noise" or if there is a statistically significant reason to pick up the phone and dispute the quote.

The Math Link

In formal terms, the Chi-Square (χ2\chi^2χ2) statistic measures the normalized squared deviation between observed frequencies and expected frequencies under a null hypothesis H0H_0H0​.

Let O\mathcal{O}O be the set of observed frequencies and E\mathcal{E}E be the set of expected frequencies for kkk mutually exclusive categories. The Chi-Square statistic is defined as:

χ2=∑i=1k(Oi−Ei)2Ei\chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}χ2=∑i=1k​Ei​(Oi​−Ei​)2​

Where:

  • Oi∈OO_i \in \mathcal{O}Oi​∈O represents the observed frequency for category iii.
  • Ei∈EE_i \in \mathcal{E}Ei​∈E represents the expected frequency for category iii, calculated as Ei=n⋅piE_i = n \cdot p_iEi​=n⋅pi​ for a sample size nnn and hypothesized probability pip_ipi​.
  • kkk is the number of categories.

To determine if the result is significant, we compare χ2\chi^2χ2 to a distribution with degrees of freedom df=k−1df = k - 1df=k−1 (for Goodness of Fit) or df=(r−1)(c−1)df = (r-1)(c-1)df=(r−1)(c−1) (for Independence, where rrr and ccc are rows and columns in a contingency table).

The logic follows a squared distance metric:

  1. The Residual (Oi−Ei)(O_i - E_i)(Oi​−Ei​): The raw difference between your insurance quote and your expectation.
  2. Squaring (Oi−Ei)2(O_i - E_i)^2(Oi​−Ei​)2: This ensures that "underpaying" and "overpaying" both contribute positively to the total error, preventing differences from canceling each other out.
  3. Normalization (÷Ei)(\div E_i)(÷Ei​): This scales the error. A $500 discrepancy matters more on a $1,000 policy than on a $10,000 commercial fleet policy.

THE INTUITION Think of χ2\chi^2χ2 as a "surprise meter." If the value is near zero, your data is exactly what you predicted. As the value climbs, your "surprise" increases. Once the value crosses a specific threshold (the critical value), the surprise is so great that you must reject your original assumptions.

The Frequency Fallacy: Chi-Square MUST be performed on raw frequency counts, never on percentages or ratios. If you run a Chi-Square on percentages (e.g., using 0.70 instead of 70), the denominator EiE_iEi​ will be tiny, blowing the χ2\chi^2χ2 value out of proportion and leading to a Type I error (false positive).

ML Applications

  • Feature Selection: In supervised learning, the Chi-Square test is used to rank categorical features. By measuring the independence between a feature and the target label, we can discard features that provide zero information gain.
  • Deciding Discretization Bins: When converting continuous variables into categorical "bins," Chi-Square analysis helps determine if the bin boundaries maintain the statistical relationship with the target variable.
  • Model Calibration Checks: Used to evaluate if the predicted probabilities of a classifier match the observed frequencies of the classes (e.g., if a model predicts 10% probability of churn, do 10 out of 100 samples actually churn?).
  • Data Drift Detection: In MLOps, Chi-Square is used to compare the distribution of categorical features in production data vs. training data. A high χ2\chi^2χ2 score triggers an alert that the model’s environment has changed.
  • Image Histogram Comparison: While often replaced by Earth Mover's Distance, Chi-Square is a classic method for comparing the distribution of pixel intensities (stored as frequency bins) between two images for similarity detection.

Debugging Tip: If your expected frequency EiE_iEi​ in any cell is less than 5, the Chi-Square test becomes unreliable. In these cases, your "insurance audit" is underpowered. You should either increase your sample size or use Fisher’s Exact Test instead.
